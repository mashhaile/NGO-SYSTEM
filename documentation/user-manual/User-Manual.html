Part A

Getting started

Signing in, finding your way around, and the order of work.

1. About the system

The NGO Management System keeps all of an organization's program work in one place: the people it serves, its programs and projects, activities and trainings, the goods and services it delivers, its donors and donations, its budgets and spending, and the reports that bring all of this together.

What you can do with it

  • Register beneficiaries (one at a time or from Excel), enroll them in programs, and group them in enterprises.
  • Plan the work: programs, projects (also in phases), terms of reference, activities and activity runs.
  • Record the work: attendance at trainings, and the goods, cash and services delivered to people, households, groups and institutions.
  • Manage money: donors, donations in several currencies, approved budgets, and expenses with approval.
  • Report: a dashboard and ready-made reports for any period, with Excel, PDF and CSV export.

Your organization's data is private

Many organizations use the same system, but each one sees only its own records. Within your organization, each user sees and does only what their role allows (see chapter 2).

Words in this manual

Menu paths are written like Beneficiaries › Enrollments: open the Beneficiaries group in the left menu, then click Enrollments. Buttons are written in bold. Your administrator may have renamed some terms (for example "Fund" instead of "Donation"), so your screens can use different words from this manual (see chapter 24).

2. Signing in and passwords

Signing in

  1. Open https://ngo-system-e5af.onrender.com.
  2. Enter your Email and Password and click Sign in. The eye icon shows the password as you type.
  3. The Dashboard opens.
  4. To leave, open the menu under your name at the top right and choose Logout. Always log out on a shared computer.
First visit of the day: the site may need up to one minute to start. After that it is fast.

Passwords

  • A password needs at least 8 characters, with letters and numbers, and must not be your email address.
  • After 5 wrong passwords the account is locked for 15 minutes. It unlocks by itself, or an administrator can reset the password.
  • First sign-in: you sign in with the password your administrator gave you, and the system asks you to choose your own. Only you know it.
  • After that, your organization administrator manages your password. Forgot it? Ask them to reset it: you get a temporary password and choose your own again at the next sign-in.

User types (roles)

RoleWhat it can do
Organization AdminEverything in the organization: settings, users, roles, wording, lookups, custom fields, all records, all approvals.
Finance OfficerBudgets and expenses, including approving them; donors and donations.
Data EntryDaily records: beneficiaries, enrollments, enterprises, projects, terms of reference (writing and submitting), activity runs, attendance, service deliveries, donations, expenses (submitted for approval).
ViewerCan see everything and run reports, but cannot change anything.

Your administrator can also create custom roles such as "Program Manager" or "M&E Officer" with exactly the rights your organization needs (chapter 23). The menu shows only what your role may use.

3. Finding your way around

The screen

  • Left menu: the modules, in groups (Main, Beneficiaries, Services, Programs, Activities, Funding, Finance, Administration). Click a group title to fold or unfold it.
  • Top bar: the bell shows notifications (for example "a budget is waiting for your approval"). Your name and role are at the top right.

Lists

Each module opens with a list of its records.

  • Search finds records by name, code or other text. Filters (status, program, year …) narrow the list. Reset clears them.
  • Click a name (green) to open the record. The icons on the right are view, edit and delete.
  • The button at the top right adds a new record (for example Add Beneficiary, Create Activity, New Budget).

Forms

  • Fields marked * are required.
  • Lists that depend on each other open in order: choose the Program first, then its Project; choose the Region, then its Zone, then its Woreda.
  • If something is wrong, a message at the top says "Please check: …" and names the field and the problem.

Detail pages

A detail page shows everything about one record and the records linked to it (for example a project's phases, activities and budget). Use Edit to change it and Back to return to the list.

Codes and history

  • The system gives every record a code automatically, for example BEN-000123 for a beneficiary or PRG-000002-PRJ-003-ACT-001-RUN-002 for an activity run (the code shows where the run belongs). Use codes when you talk about a record.
  • Every change is saved immediately and recorded in the history, which shows who did what and when.
  • A record that others depend on cannot be deleted. For example, a project with activities cannot be deleted. The message tells you why.

Statuses of work

Programs, projects, activities and activity runs all use the same five statuses:

Part B

Working with the modules

One chapter per menu item, in the order of the left menu.

5. Dashboard

Everyone

The dashboard opens after you sign in. It shows your organization's key figures at a glance: beneficiaries, programs, donors, funding received (per currency), services delivered and their value, followed by charts about beneficiaries (gender, age, location), programs and services.

The dashboard shows the current picture. For a chosen period, filters or an export, use Reports (chapter 20).

6. Beneficiaries

Admin · Data Entry (add and edit) · others view

The people your organization serves, with their personal, contact, location and vulnerability information. A beneficiary is registered once and can then be enrolled in programs, take part in activities and receive services.

Add a beneficiary

  1. Go to Beneficiaries › Beneficiaries and click Add Beneficiary.
  2. Personal Information: First, Middle and Last Name, Gender, Date of Birth (the age is calculated for you) and Marital Status.
  3. Contact Information: Phone, Alternative Phone, Email.
  4. Address Information: Country, then Region › Zone › Woreda (each list opens after the previous one is chosen), Kebele and Address.
  5. Socio Economic and Vulnerability Information: Education Level, Occupation, Person With Disability, Disability Type and Vulnerable Groups.
  6. Custom Fields: any extra questions your organization added.
  7. Click Save. The beneficiary gets a number such as BEN-000501.

Import many beneficiaries from Excel

  1. On the beneficiary list click Import Excel, then Download Template.
  2. Fill in one row per person. Use the official region, zone and woreda names. Do not type the age: it is calculated from the date of birth.
  3. Upload the file (.xlsx, at most 10 MB). The system imports the correct rows and lists the rows with problems so you can fix and upload them again.

The beneficiary page

Open a beneficiary to see all their information: personal, contact, address, socio-economic, vulnerability, custom fields and documents. Use Edit Beneficiary to change details. To see the programs, trainings and services a person received, use the Beneficiaries report (chapter 20).

Status

  • Active: normal.
  • Inactive: the person has left (moved, passed away, no longer served). Their history stays, but they cannot be enrolled, receive services or be added to anything new.

7. Enrollments

Admin · Data Entry

An enrollment records that a beneficiary is part of a program, from a date, with a status. Reports use enrollments to count the people enrolled in each program.

Enroll a beneficiary

  1. Go to Beneficiaries › Enrollments and click Create Enrollment.
  2. Choose the Beneficiary and the Program, and enter the Enrollment Date (inside the program's dates).
  3. Set the Status and add Remarks if needed, then save.
StatusUse when
PendingSelected but not started yet
ActiveTaking part
SuspendedPaused for a while
CompletedFinished the program: enter the Completion Date
DroppedLeft before finishing: enter the Exit Reason
A person can be enrolled in a program only once, and only while the program is not Completed, Suspended or Cancelled.

8. Enterprises and members

Admin · Data Entry

Enterprises are businesses, cooperatives and groups run by beneficiaries, for example a dairy cooperative or a women's savings group. Their members are beneficiaries.

Add an enterprise

  1. Go to Beneficiaries › Enterprises and click Add Enterprise.
  2. Enter the Enterprise Name, Enterprise Type, Ownership Type, Business Sector, Business Status, Registration Number, Capital, Start Date, contact and location, then save.

Add members

  1. Open the enterprise and use Add Enterprise Member in its Members table (or Enterprise Members in the menu).
  2. Choose the Beneficiary, the Member Role (Member, Partner, Shareholder, Manager, Employee) and the Joined Date.
  3. When a member leaves, edit the membership and enter the Left Date.
An enterprise is Active or Inactive. No new members can be added to an inactive enterprise or from inactive beneficiaries.

9. Programs

Admin (others view)

A program is a large area of work, such as "WASH and Climate Resilience" or "Child Protection and Inclusion". Projects, enrollments, service deliveries, donations and budgets all belong to a program.

  1. Go to Programs › Programs and click Add Program.
  2. Enter the Program Name, Category, Description and Target Beneficiaries.
  3. Set the Start Date and End Date, and the Status (see chapter 4). Save.

The program page shows its projects, budget and spending, and the people it reached.

A program's dates cannot be shortened so that its projects or enrollments fall outside them.

10. Projects and phases

Admin · Data Entry

A project is a piece of work inside a program, usually with its own donor, manager, dates and budget.

Add a project

  1. Go to Programs › Projects and click Add Project.
  2. Choose the Program and enter the Project Name and Description. Choose the Donor and the Project Manager.
  3. Enter the Start Date and End Date (inside the program's dates) and the Status. Save.

The project's money is not entered on this form. It is planned in Finance › Budgets (chapter 18) and then shows on the project page.

Projects in phases

When a project runs in phases (Phase 1 in 2026, Phase 2 in 2027 …), each phase is created as its own project linked to the main project:

  1. Open the main project.
  2. In the Phases card click Add Phase. Give it a name and dates inside the main project's dates.
  3. Each phase then has its own activities, budget, deliveries and reports, just like a single project. The main project's page and reports add up all its phases.

11. Terms of Reference (TOR)

Data Entry writes · Admin approves

A TOR describes a planned activity or training before it happens: why, what, who, where, how and at what cost. After approval, the activity can be created directly from it.

Write and submit a TOR

  1. Go to Activities › Terms of Reference and click New TOR.
  2. TOR Information: Title, Project, Activity Type, and an existing Activity or Activity Run if it is for one (usually left empty).
  3. Purpose: Background, Objectives and Expected Outputs (all three are required before submitting).
  4. Participants and Facilitators: who will attend, the expected number, and facilitators or resource persons.
  5. Date and Venue: start and end dates, region, woreda and venue.
  6. Methodology, Logistics and Reporting, and the Budget lines (description, amount, currency).
  7. Save, then click Submit for Approval.

Approve

The approver opens the TOR and clicks Approve, or Return it with a comment. A returned TOR can be corrected and submitted again. The Approval History shows who submitted and approved it and when. Use Download PDF to print or share it.

Create the activity from the TOR

On an approved TOR, click Create Activity from TOR. The activity is filled in from the TOR (name, type, project, dates, place).

12. Activities

Admin (Data Entry through an approved TOR)

An activity is something a project does: a training, workshop, meeting, campaign, field visit, assessment, distribution and so on. An activity can run several times (see Activity runs).

  1. Go to Activities › Activities and click Create Activity (or create it from an approved TOR).
  2. Activity Information: Project, Activity Name, Activity Type, Participant Source (enrolled beneficiaries, staff, partners, mixed …), Target Participants, Target Groups, Facilitator and the Terms of Reference.
  3. Date and Location: Activity Date (inside the project's dates), Region, Woreda and Venue / Site.
  4. Add a Description or Remarks, set the Status and save.

13. Activity runs

Admin · Data Entry

An activity run is one implementation of an activity: one batch, in one place, on certain dates. For example, "Business skills training" may have Batch 1 in Adama and Batch 2 in Adigrat. Attendance is recorded per run.

  1. Go to Activities › Activity Runs and click Create Activity Run.
  2. Choose the Activity. Enter the Run Name, Trainer and Target Group.
  3. Schedule and Location: Start Date and End Date (inside the project's dates), Region, Woreda and Venue / Site.
  4. Add Notes, set the Status and save. The run gets a code such as …-ACT-001-RUN-002.

14. Attendance

Admin · Data Entry

Attendance records who took part in each session of an activity run. It feeds the reports on people trained, completion and attendance rate.

Create a session

  1. Go to Activities › Attendance and click Create Attendance.
  2. Choose the Activity Run, enter the Session Number, Session Title and Session Date (inside the run's dates), and save.

Fill in the attendance sheet

  1. Open the session's sheet (the sheet icon in the list).
  2. With Beneficiary selected as the Participant Type, pick a person in Select Beneficiary and click Add. Repeat for each person.
  3. To add others, choose their Participant Type (Staff, Board, Partner, Government, Community, Other), enter their name, gender, age group, organization, position and phone, and click Add.
  4. In Attendance Records, set each person's status: Present, Late, Excused or Absent, then save. The totals at the top (participants, present, absent, late) update.
Two people editing the same sheet: if someone else saved the sheet after you opened it, your save is refused so that their work is not overwritten. Reload the sheet and enter your changes again.

15. Service delivery

Admin · Data Entry

A service delivery records goods, cash or services given out: what, how much, to whom, where, when, and which donation paid for it. Reports use it to count the people reached and the value delivered.

Who receives it

Recipient TypeAllocationExample
IndividualIndividual: each person's own quantity is entered.
Shared: the total is shared equally by the people chosen.		Hygiene kits to 20 women; a training shared by its participants
HouseholdTo the household as a wholeFood package per household
Group / CommunityTo the group as a wholeWater point for a community
InstitutionTo the institution as a wholeFurniture for a school or health post

Record a delivery

  1. Go to Services › Service Delivery and click Create Service Delivery.
  2. Choose the Recipient Type and Allocation Method, then the Program and, if it belongs to them, the Project, Activity and Activity Run.
  3. Choose the Beneficiaries (or the household, group or institution) and the Service Delivery Date.
  4. Delivery Location: Region › Zone › Woreda and Venue / Site.
  5. Service Items: for each item, the Service Category, Service Type, item, Unit, Quantity, Unit Cost and Currency. Add details (for example what a kit contains) if needed.
  6. Funding: choose the Donation that paid for each item. If the item was bought with an expense, choose it in Bought with expense so that the cost is not counted twice.
  7. Check the allocation per person and save.

The delivery page

Shows the overview (people reached, value), the items, how they were allocated to each person, and the donations and expenses that paid for them.

Only services allowed for the chosen recipient type are offered (set by the administrator in Lookups). Inactive beneficiaries and suspended or cancelled programs, projects and activities cannot receive new deliveries. A delivery that did not happen should be set to Cancelled; it is then left out of the reports.

16. Donors

Admin · Finance Officer

Donors and funding partners: governments, foundations, NGOs, companies and individuals.

  1. Go to Funding › Donors and click Add Donor.
  2. Basic Information: Donor Name, Donor Type (Individual, Organization, Government, NGO, Corporate, Foundation), Registration Number, Status.
  3. Contact Information: Contact Person, Phone, Email, Website, Country, Address.
  4. Donor Dates: Donor Since, Last Contact Date, Next Follow-up Date, and Notes.
  5. Reporting and Privacy: tick Share beneficiary names with this donor only if the donor may see names. Otherwise they see numbers only.

Share results with a donor (share link)

On the donor's page you can create a private link that lets the donor see where their donations went (people reached, places, services), without an account. Choose all donations or one, and how long the link is valid (90 days recommended). You can revoke a link at any time, and the page shows when it was last viewed.

17. Donations

Admin · Finance Officer · Data Entry (add)

Money and in-kind support received or promised by donors, and what each donation is allocated to.

Record a donation

  1. Go to Funding › Donations and click Add Donation.
  2. Choose the Donor and enter the Donation Title, Donation Type (Cash, Bank Transfer, Material, Equipment, Service, Other), Funding Source and Funding Restriction (Restricted or Unrestricted).
  3. Enter the Amount, Currency and Donation Date. For a currency other than ETB, also enter the Exchange rate when received.
  4. Choose the Program and, if any, the Project it funds, and enter the Receipt Number and Reference Number.
  5. Set the Status: Pledged (promised), Received, or Cancelled. Save.

Where the donation went

Open a donation and click Where Did It Go? to see what it paid for: expenses, items given out, places and people reached, and how much is left. For a foreign currency it also shows the value in ETB (received, used, exchange gain or loss). Download Donor Statement gives a PDF for the donor.

Possible double counting: when items were given out and an expense on the same donation has about the same amount but they are not linked, the list shows a warning. Open it and link the items to the expense, or confirm they are different. Untraced spending lists deliveries that are not linked to any donation.

18. Budgets

Admin · Finance Officer

Budgets plan the money per year and per cost category. A program budget sets the total per year; project budgets plan the details and draw on their program's budget. Expenses are charged to the lines of approved project budgets.

Create a budget

  1. Go to Finance › Budgets and click New Budget.
  2. Choose Project budget or Program budget, then the project or program, the Budget name, the Currency and the Period (From FY … To FY …; one or several years).
  3. Budget Lines: for each line, the Category (Personnel, Training & Workshops, Travel & Per Diem, Direct Beneficiary Support, Supplies & Materials, Equipment, Consultancy & Services, Administration & Overhead, Other), a description and the amount for each year.
  4. Totals shown in red go over what is left in the program budget. Save.
  5. Click Submit for Approval. The budget is locked while it is reviewed, and the approvers are notified.

Approve or return

A finance officer or admin opens the budget and clicks Approve, or Return it with a comment. A returned budget can be corrected and submitted again.

Change an approved budget (revision)

  1. Open the approved budget and start a revision.
  2. Change the figures and give the Reason for this revision (what changed and why).
  3. Click Submit Revision. Once approved, the old figures are kept as a version in the History, and expenses stay attached. Cancel Revision discards the proposal.

Spent = approved expenses. Pending = expenses recorded but not yet approved (not deducted yet). For a project in phases, each phase has its own budget.

19. Expenses

Data Entry / Finance record · Finance Officer / Admin approve

Actual spending, charged to a line of an approved project budget and, if known, paid from a donation.

Record an expense

  1. Go to Finance › Expenses and click Record Expense.
  2. Charge To: choose the approved Project budget, the Expense date (its fiscal year is shown) and the Budget line.
  3. Expense Details: Description, Paid to (payee), Amount, Currency, Payment method (Cash, Bank Transfer, Cheque, Mobile Money, Other) and Receipt / invoice no.
  4. Links (optional): the Activity it was for, and the donation it was Paid from.
  5. Save, then Submit for Approval.

Approve, return or void

  • Approve Expense: it now counts as spent on the budget line and is locked.
  • Return for Changes: it goes back to the person who recorded it.
  • Over budget: if the line would go over budget, the form warns you. It can still be submitted, but the approver must click Approve over budget and give a reason.
  • Void Expense: cancels an approved expense that was wrong (with a reason). It then no longer counts as spent but stays in the history.

Everyone (money figures only for roles that may see finance)

  5. Download with Excel, PDF or CSV.
The report address in the browser keeps the period and filters, so you can bookmark a report or send its link to a colleague who has access.

21. Organization settings

Organization Admin

Administration › Organization holds your organization's details: Basic Information (name, type, description, founded year), Legal Information (legal name, registration and tax numbers), Contact Information, Primary Contact Person, Address, and Branding (logo). Keep them up to date: they appear on reports and statements.

22. Users

Organization Admin
  1. Go to Administration › User Management and click Create User.
  2. Enter the Full Name, Email and Role, and a First password that follows the rules in chapter 2.
  3. Give the person their email and that first password. At their first sign-in they must choose their own, so only they know it.
  • A user forgot their password: use Reset password. The system creates a temporary password (use Copy to copy it), and the user chooses a new one at their next sign-in. Users cannot change their password themselves after the first sign-in.
  • An administrator forgot theirs: another administrator of the organization, or the platform administrator, resets it.
  • Locked users (too many wrong passwords) unlock after 15 minutes, or when you reset their password.
  • Deactivate User when someone leaves: they can no longer sign in, but their work stays in the records and history.

23. Roles

Organization Admin

The four built-in roles (chapter 2) cannot be changed. If your organization needs different rights, make your own roles:

  1. Go to Administration › Roles and click New Role, or Copy as new role next to a built-in role to start from it.
  2. Give the Role name (for example "Program Manager" or "M&E Officer") and a Description.
  3. Tick the permissions per module: view, add, edit, delete, approve.
  4. Save, then give the role to users in User Management.

24. Wording

Organization Admin

Administration › Wording lets your organization use its own words. For example, "Fund" instead of "Donation", "Administrator" instead of "Organization Admin", or "Ongoing" instead of the status "Active".

  1. Type your word (and its plural) next to the term. Leave the box empty to keep the default.
  2. Check the Preview, then click Save Wording. Menus, pages, forms, reports and exports use the new words at once.
  3. Default returns a term to its original word.

Only words are changed, never your data. Renaming a status changes only how it is shown; its meaning and rules stay the same.

25. Lookups

Organization Admin

Administration › Lookup Management holds the choices that appear in drop-down lists, by module and category: gender, education level, occupation, disability type, service categories and service types, items and units, and so on.

  1. Open a module and category and click Manage, or click Add Lookup.
  2. Enter the Value (and a Code if you use one). Codes must be unique.
  3. For service types, also set Can be given to (individuals, households, groups, institutions), the Allowed Units and the Allocation Behavior.
  4. A value that is no longer used can be deactivated. It disappears from new forms but stays on old records.

26. Custom fields

Organization Admin

Administration › Custom Fields adds extra questions to the standard forms, for example "Household size" or "Mobile money user" on the beneficiary form.

  1. Click Create Custom Field. Choose the Module (Beneficiary, Donor, Program, Service Delivery, User, Enterprise, Enterprise Member).
  2. Enter the Field Label (what users see) and Field Key (a short internal name), and choose the Field Type (text, number, date, a list of choices …).
  3. Tick Required field if it must be filled in, and Active field to show it. Save.

Appendix C: Rules the system checks

  • Dates: an end date is never before its start date. Records stay inside their parent's dates: a project inside its program, an activity and its runs inside the project, a session inside its run, an enrollment inside the program, a membership inside the enterprise's dates. A parent's dates cannot be shortened past its records. Work that is already done cannot be dated in the future.
  • Status: see chapter 4 and Appendix A.
  • Money: expenses only go to approved project budgets. A project budget cannot exceed what is left in its program budget. Items bought with an expense are not charged to the donation a second time.
  • Duplicates: a person is enrolled in a program only once. Lookup codes are unique.

Appendix D: Currencies

The system works in ETB, USD, EUR, GBP and CHF. Each amount keeps its own currency. Totals are shown per currency (for example "ETB 124,950,000 + USD 80,000"). For a donation in a foreign currency, the exchange rate on the day it was received is recorded, so reports can also show its value in ETB, the value of what was used, and the exchange gain or loss.
